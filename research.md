My doctoral research at Monash University examines the international consequences of rapid US illiberalization, asking how democratic erosion in a global hegemon is transmitted outward through the material, ideational, and security architecture that sustains its position — and how that transmission lands so differently across states depending on their place in the hegemonic hierarchy.

Alongside doctoral research, I have spent nearly a decade leading and conducting research in politically sensitive and conflict-affected environments — directing USAID-funded research portfolios in Myanmar on democratic resistance, national identity, and governance, and leading gender research across East Africa. This work has informed programmatic and policy decisions for USAID, the World Bank, and other donors and implementers. See [Publications](/publications/) and [CV](/cv/) for the full record.
