## Focus

My research examines democratic erosion and authoritarianism, with particular attention to competitive authoritarianism and the effects of U.S. autocratization on countries shaped by substantial American engagement — in aid architecture, security guarantees, and democracy assistance.

## Framework

The project treats autocratization culminating in democratic collapse as the independent variable, drawing on Lührmann and Lindberg's sequencing of third-wave autocratization, Levitsky and Ziblatt on institutional forbearance, and Ginsburg and Huq on constitutional retrogression.

Collapse is theorised as a **critical juncture** in Capoccia and Kelemen's sense — a bounded period in which structural constraints loosen and the range of viable paths for dependent states widens sharply. Ikenberry's account of liberal hegemonic order supplies the scope condition: the argument holds where American underwriting was structurally load-bearing, not merely present.

Illiberalization is the connective tissue — the mechanism carrying domestic collapse outward into the aid architecture, security guarantees, and normative commitments that shaped partner states' domestic politics.
